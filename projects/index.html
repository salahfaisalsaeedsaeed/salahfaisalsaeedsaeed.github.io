Featured B.Sc. Graduation Project

Manipulator Robot Error Correction Using Computer Vision

A six-degree-of-freedom robotic manipulator combined with external visual feedback to estimate pose error using YOLOv11 marker detection and OpenCV SolvePnP. The work integrates mechanical design, embedded control, computer vision, camera calibration, and geometric pose estimation in a single engineering framework.

Taiz University · 2025Mechatronics & Robotics Engineering
Team graduation project
6-DOFrobotic manipulator
50training epochs
4,305images in the augmented training pipeline
95.0%reported final-epoch precision
96.7%reported final-epoch recall
97.0%reported mAP@0.5

Engineering workflow

Mechanical design6-DOF arm modeled in SolidWorks with kinematic and workspace considerations.
DatasetReal marker images captured across different viewpoints and lighting conditions.
Vision modelYOLOv11 trained for detection of the three fixed “+” reference markers.
CalibrationCamera intrinsics estimated from approximately 35 checkerboard images.
Pose estimation2D detections paired with known 3D coordinates and processed with SolvePnP.
ValidationDetected and reprojected points compared to quantify pose-estimation error.

System implementation

Vision hardware
Raspberry Pi 5; Arducam HQ camera with 12.3 MP sensor and 6 mm lens mounted on Link 5.
Embedded control
ESP32, six A4988 stepper drivers, NEMA17 motors, limit/inductive sensors, emergency-stop and 12 V supply.
Software
Python, OpenCV, YOLOv11, Roboflow, Google Colab, MATLAB/engineering analysis tools, and SolidWorks.
Data split
85% training, 5% validation, and 10% testing, with rotation, noise, brightness, and shear augmentation.

Measured outcomes

mAP@0.5:0.95
84.3% at the final reported training epoch.
Reprojection
Reported below 0.02 pixels in the SolvePnP verification.
Positioning
Overall positional error reported within a few millimeters in the evaluated setup.
Application scope
Potential use in pick-and-place, assembly, medical robotics, education, and research platforms.
Validation boundary. The report explicitly states that full real-time physical correction was not completed because of hardware constraints; correction logic was validated through simulation/offline analysis. The system also depends on fixed reference markers and line of sight, and it does not yet fuse actuator/encoder feedback with the external vision loop. The physical prototype was suitable for motion testing, while full load-handling fabrication remained future work.
